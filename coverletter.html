Cover Letters

Letters that sound like me

My story points

01

Staying calm when it counts

At Atria Senior Living (2023–2024), care isn't just routine — it's being ready when routine breaks. I transferred residents safely between bed and wheelchair, handled personal care and medication reminders daily, and when an emergency came up, I acted. That instinct — stay calm, follow safety protocol, take care of the person in front of you — is the same instinct I'd bring into a classroom.

02

Six years, four employers, one throughline

From 2018 to 2025, across four different care roles — Premier Health System, BAYADA Home Care, Atria Senior Living, and Home Instead — medication reminders were a constant on my plate. Every employer trusted me with the same responsibility: making sure people got what they needed, on time, without fail. That's not a coincidence — it's a pattern I built on purpose.

03

Learning fast, on purpose

While working full-time in caregiving, I completed a 4-month accelerated Pharmacy Technician program and, separately, an 8-week Medical Office Training program — both remote, both while still showing up for clients. I didn't slow down to build new skills; I built them alongside the work. That's the same energy I bring to picking up a new classroom, a new student's needs, a new routine.

04

A language beyond English

I bring [French / Haitian Creole — confirm which] into my work — a real asset for connecting with students and families from different backgrounds.

05

Starting over, on purpose

My path started in Haiti, where I earned my GED at Collège Ruben Leconte, Port-au-Prince. Rebuilding a life, a career, and a set of credentials in a new country takes the same resilience and adaptability I bring to every new client, every new role, every new challenge. Starting over isn't something I fear — it's something I've already proven I can do well.
